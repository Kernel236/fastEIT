    Preprocessing (layer 2)¶

    pipeline ¶

    preprocess(): from the parsed frames to a recording ready for analysis (layer 2).

    Fixed order of the steps (notebooks/05_preprocessing_choices.ipynb): 1. time in seconds, pixel copy with the device "no data" values as NaN; 2. frames with missing pixel data filled and excluded; 3. respiratory rate from the spectrum, cardiac step (MDN with a heart rate given by the user, otherwise only a moving average); 4. breath detection on the global signal (sum of all pixels); 5. quality flags and exclusions of each breath; 6. lung mask from the valid breaths, regions of interest, regional signals; 7. auxiliary signals (pressures, flow, volume, device markers) with "no data" as NaN, in the same units for every vendor (cmH2O, L/s, mL); 8. airway pressures of each breath: PEEP, plateau, driving pressure (notebooks/06_peep_trial_segmentation.ipynb); 9. inspiratory tidal volume of each breath, from the ventilator curves.

    Every step writes what it did in EITRecording.log (saved with the results) and in the Python logger fasteit.preprocessing.pipeline (technical log, configured by the application, not here).

    recording_time ¶

    recording_time(raw: ReconstructedFrameData) -> np.ndarray

    Time of each frame in seconds from the start of the recording.

    Draeger .bin timestamps are a fraction of the day: they are converted to seconds and a recording that crosses midnight is unwrapped. Other vendors already give seconds.

    cardiac_filter ¶

    Cardiac filtering of the pixel signals.

    Policy (notebooks/05_preprocessing_choices.ipynb, section 5c): - heart rate given by the user -> MDN filter; - no heart rate -> only a light moving average, the cardiac component is NOT removed.

    All functions work along axis 0 (time). Rates are in Hz here; the configuration uses breaths or beats per minute, and the caller converts with SECONDS_PER_MINUTE. Parameters have no defaults here on purpose: they live only in fasteit.config.PreprocessingConfig.

    Valid for adult recordings only (respiratory rate 6-60/min); not for neonates, paediatric patients or high-frequency oscillation.

    respiratory_rate ¶

    respiratory_rate(global_signal: ndarray, fs: float, min_bpm: float, max_bpm: float, window_seconds: float) -> float

    Respiratory rate (Hz) from the power spectrum of the global signal.

    Same method as the respiratory part of eitprocessing RateDetection (v1.8.8; Somhorst et al., JOSS 2026;11(117):8179, DOI: 10.21105/joss.08179): Welch spectrum (Hann window, 50 % overlap, mean removed), highest peak between min_bpm and max_bpm, refined with a parabola through the peak and its two neighbours.

    It is the mean rate of the whole signal, not of single breaths. In the evidence notebook (section 4) the largest difference from the device breath markers was 1.7 breaths/min over 11 recordings (rates 10-42/min). With a 30 s window the spectral bins are 2/min apart before the refinement.

    mdn_filter ¶

    mdn_filter(signals: ndarray, fs: float, respiratory_rate_hz: float, heart_rate_hz: float, notch_half_width_hz: float, upper_limit_hz: float, order: int) -> np.ndarray

    Remove the cardiac component with a multiple digital notch (MDN) filter.

    Wisse et al., "Improved filtering methods to suppress cardiovascular contamination in EIT recordings", Physiol Meas 2024, DOI: 10.1088/1361-6579/ad46e3. Implementation follows eitprocessing MDNFilter (v1.8.8):

    1. one band-stop (Butterworth, zero phase) around each cardiac harmonic k * heart_rate ± notch_half_width, up to upper_limit_hz;
    2. the first band-stop never starts below (respiratory_rate + heart_rate) / 2, so that it does not cut the breathing;
    3. a low-pass at upper_limit_hz removes what is left above it.

    Zero-phase filtering (forward and backward) doubles the effective order of each filter. Apply it to the whole recording, not to short pieces: narrow band-stops of this order have transients of several seconds at both ends.

    The heart rate comes from the user (section 5c of the evidence notebook). It is a snapshot: a heart rate drifting more than about ±10/min during the recording, or an arrhythmia such as atrial fibrillation, is not removed by fixed notches. A heart rate above upper_limit_hz + notch_half_width_hz (about 230/min) gets no notch, only the low-pass.

    		 required

    Highest frequency filtered, in Hz.

    exclusions ¶

    Parts of a recording excluded from the analysis.

    Exclusions come from the user (source="manual") or from the device (source="device": frames with a timing error or with missing pixel data). They are widened by a margin, because the forward-backward filters spread an artifact over the neighbouring samples, and a breath that touches an exclusion is marked excluded: it stays in the breath table (fastEIT design decision D5).

    Frames with missing pixel data are also filled with the nearest valid frame, so that the filters can run on a continuous signal; the filled frames are excluded.

    breaths ¶

    Breath detection on the global impedance signal.

    Reimplementation of eitprocessing BreathDetection (v1.8.8; Somhorst et al., JOSS 2026;11(117):8179, DOI: 10.21105/joss.08179). It gives the same breaths as eitprocessing on 11 regular real recordings (notebooks/05_preprocessing_choices.ipynb, section 8) and on synthetic signals with a spike and with small breaths. This shows equivalence with eitprocessing, not accuracy against a gold standard.

    One change: breaths close to invalid data are flagged, not removed.

    A breath is a triplet of frame indices: start (valley), peak, end (next valley). Impedance is assumed to rise with inspiration (as in the Draeger .bin pixels).

    Limitations (to keep in mind when reading Ti, Te and the respiratory rate): - the valley is the minimum of impedance, i.e. of lung volume, not the start of the inspiratory effort or the ventilator trigger; the peak includes any inspiratory pause. Timings are impedance-based and not interchangeable with the ventilator values; - patient-ventilator asynchrony is not detected: an ineffective effort is usually not a peak, and two inspirations without a valley between them become one breath; - the 15 s baseline follows slow EELI drift, but after a sudden EELI step (PEEP change, recruitment) the breaths within about 15 s of the step are unreliable.

    invalid_samples ¶

    invalid_samples(signal: ndarray, percentile: float, multiplier: float) -> np.ndarray

    Indices of the gross outliers of the signal (step 1).

    A sample is invalid when it is below mean - multiplier * (mean - low) or above mean + multiplier * (high - mean), where low and high are the percentile and 100 - percentile percentiles of the signal. With the defaults (5, 4) only gross outliers are caught: artefacts such as a disconnection spike, but also extreme manoeuvres such as a sustained inflation or a large recruitment step, which are physiological.

    breath_quality ¶

    Quality flags of the detected breaths.

    Flags mark a breath, they never remove it: the clinician decides (fastEIT design decision D5). Thresholds and the evidence for them are in notebooks/05_preprocessing_choices.ipynb, section 8; they are fastEIT choices, not taken from a paper.

    All measures come from the global impedance signal, so Ti and Ttot are impedance-based (valley to peak, valley to valley) and not the ventilator values; the amplitude is in arbitrary impedance units, not a volume.

    breath_measures ¶

    lung_mask ¶

    Functional lung mask with the watershed method.

    Reimplementation of eitprocessing WatershedLungspace (v1.8.8; Somhorst et al., JOSS 2026;11(117):8179, DOI: 10.21105/joss.08179), with the pixel amplitude in its "phase shift" mode (PixelBreath) and the small-region filter FilterROIBySize. Checked to give the same masks as eitprocessing on 11 real recordings when every breath is used (notebooks/05_preprocessing_choices.ipynb, sections 7b and 9).

    The mask is the VENTILATED lung, not the anatomical lung: a collapsed region with a TIV below the threshold is outside it.

    Steps: 1. TIV map: pixel value at the end of inspiration of the global signal minus the value at its start, mean over the breaths; 2. amplitude map: the same, but on each pixel's own timing, found by cross-correlation with the global signal (it catches pixels that inflate late, e.g. pendelluft); 3. threshold: a fraction of the maximum of the TIV map, applied to both maps; 4. watershed: the amplitude map is split into regions around its local maxima; a region is kept if its maximum is inside the TIV mask. Mask = kept regions inside the amplitude mask; 5. islands smaller than a minimum size are removed.

    The watershed assumes smooth images, with high amplitude in the centre of the lung and low amplitude at its edges, as reconstructed EIT images are.

    regions ¶

    Regions of interest inside the lung mask.

    Orientation of Draeger images (PulmoVista 500 instructions for use): seen from the feet, like a CT slice. Row 0 is ventral; column 0 is the patient's RIGHT, column 31 the patient's left. Consistent with the power at the heart rate per pixel (notebooks/05_preprocessing_choices.ipynb, section 9). Verified on Draeger only.

    Regions (section 9): - ventral/dorsal halves and four layers are cut on the vertical extent of the lung (first to last row of the mask), not on the image, so "dorsal" is always the dorsal part of that lung wherever the lungs sit in the image. Same approach as the createSummersROIs.m script of the original MATLAB analysis; the published source of this approach is still to be found; - right/left halves are split at the image midline (between columns 15 and 16), the sternum-spine axis with the belt centred on the patient; - quadrants = ventral/dorsal x right/left; - every region is intersected with the lung mask.

    Limit: these regions are relative to the VENTILATED lung. If the dorsal lung collapses, its pixels leave the mask and "dorsal" becomes the dorsal half of what is still ventilated, so the ventral/dorsal split hides part of a ventral shift of ventilation. Indices meant to show a shift of ventilation (centre of ventilation, dorsal fraction, silent spaces) must be computed in image coordinates, not on these regions.

    Not the same as ReconstructedFrameData.roi_signals (layer 1), which are four fixed strips of 8 image rows each.

    breath_pressures ¶

    Airway pressures of each breath: PEEP, plateau and driving pressure.

    Read on the airway pressure curve (cmH2O), all from the same channel so that a constant offset of the sensor cancels in the driving pressure (notebooks/06_peep_trial_segmentation.ipynb, sections 2 and 4). The PEEP of a breath is the end-expiratory pressure at the airway opening: the PEEP applied by the ventilator, without intrinsic PEEP (that would need an end-expiratory occlusion), so it is a lower bound of the total PEEP; with intrinsic PEEP the driving pressure is overestimated and compliance underestimated. A breath whose pressure is missing (NaN) near its start gets NaN.

    plateau_pressures ¶

    plateau_pressures(paw: ndarray, fs: float, onsets: ndarray, peeps: ndarray, window: tuple[float, float]) -> np.ndarray

    Plateau pressure of each breath, read at the end of the end-inspiratory hold.

    Between the start of inspiration of a breath and the next one: after the highest part of the breath (the peak in volume control, the flat pressure in pressure control), the start of expiration is the steepest fall of the pressure in the 0.3 s before it comes back within 2 of the next PEEP; the plateau is the median pressure from window[0] to window[1] seconds before it (notebook 06, section 4).

    A valid plateau needs at least 0.5 s between the highest part and the expiration: an end-inspiratory hold with zero flow in volume control (continuous pause, or an inspiratory hold done by hand), or the flat inspiratory pressure in pressure control, which is a plateau only if the flow is zero at the end of inspiration. Otherwise the plateau is NaN: the reading would fall on the relaxation after the peak, and the peak pressure is never used instead because it includes the resistive pressure.

    tidal_volume ¶

    Tidal volume of each breath from the ventilator curves (mL).

    The inspiratory tidal volume: in volume control it is the volume delivered, the one set on the ventilator. It is read on the volume curve, or on the flow curve by integrating the inspiratory flow, in a window around the inspiration of each EIT breath. It is the volume measured by the ventilator: with a leak (non-invasive ventilation, a leaking cuff) it includes the leak, and without compensation of the compressible volume of the circuit it includes that too.

    Tested on synthetic curves only: the recordings available have no volume or flow curve. Inspiratory flow is taken as positive, as recorded by the ventilators.
